# Deck: M10 — Bioenergetics & the ATP economy
tags: m10 bioenergetics metabolism

Q: What's the difference between ΔG°′ and the actual ΔG in a cell, and which sets direction?
A: ΔG°′ is the biochemical standard (pH 7, 1 M, 25 °C). Actual ΔG = ΔG°′ + RT ln Q depends on real concentrations and is what sets direction — often very different from ΔG°′.

---

Q: Why is ATP's actual hydrolysis ΔG (~−50) more negative than its ΔG°′ (−30.5)?
A: The cell keeps [ATP] high and [ADP]/[Pᵢ] low → small Q → RT ln Q is strongly negative. Far-from-equilibrium gives extra driving force.

---

Q: List the structural reasons ATP hydrolysis is favourable.
A: Charge-repulsion relief (~4 crammed negatives), resonance stabilisation of released Pᵢ, entropy increase (1 molecule → 2), better solvation of products. Not a special "high-energy bond."

---

Q: What is phosphoryl-transfer potential, and where does ATP sit?
A: −ΔG°′ of a compound's hydrolysis. Phosphate flows high→low. Order: PEP > 1,3-BPG > creatine-P > ATP > glucose-6-P > glycerol-3-P. ATP is deliberately MID-range.

---

Q: Why does ATP being mid-range in the transfer hierarchy make it a good currency?
A: Higher-potential donors (e.g. PEP) can recharge ADP→ATP; ATP can phosphorylate lower-potential acceptors (e.g. glucose). Mid-position = two-way universal intermediary.

---

Q: Is ATP the cell's energy storage molecule?
A: No — it's a rapidly turned-over transfer intermediate (cash flow; ~body weight regenerated per day). Storage is fat and glycogen (savings).

---

Q: NADH vs NADPH — same chemistry, so what's the difference in role?
A: NADP⁺/NADPH carries an extra phosphate tag. Cell keeps NAD⁺/NADH high (poised to ACCEPT e⁻ → catabolism) and NADPH/NADP⁺ high (poised to DONATE e⁻ → anabolism/biosynthesis).

---

Q: Relate free energy to redox potential.
A: ΔG°′ = −nF·ΔE°′, with ΔE°′ = E°′(acceptor) − E°′(donor), F ≈ 96.5 kJ·mol⁻¹·V⁻¹. Electrons flow from low to high E°′; positive ΔE → negative ΔG → spontaneous.

---

Q: Compute the driving force for electrons going from NADH (−0.32 V) to O₂ (+0.82 V).
A: ΔE°′ = 0.82 − (−0.32) = 1.14 V → ΔG°′ = −nFΔE = −2 × 96.5 × 1.14 ≈ −220 kJ/mol. Strongly spontaneous (the basis of the electron transport chain).

---

Q: What does acetyl-CoA carry, and where does it feed?
A: An activated 2-carbon acetyl group via a high-energy thioester bond; the central hub feeding carbon into the TCA cycle.

---

Q: Catabolism vs anabolism — redox direction, and which currencies each uses?
A: Catabolism: oxidative (strips e⁻), releases energy → makes ATP + NADH. Anabolism: reductive (adds e⁻), consumes ATP + NADPH. Shared currencies link them.

---

Q: Name the cell's main activated carriers and what each shuttles.
A: ATP (phosphoryl groups / energy), NADH & NADPH (electrons / reducing power), acetyl-CoA (acyl groups). Each kept "charged" far from equilibrium.
