# Deck: M2 — Free energy & equilibrium
tags: m2 thermodynamics foundations

Q: What quantity does a system minimise at constant T and P — and what's the equation?
A: Free energy, G = H − TS. Not energy alone. H = enthalpy (bonds), S = entropy (arrangements), T = temperature (the exchange rate).

---

Q: Why doesn't a favourable reaction (HA lower energy) go fully to completion?
A: Systems minimise free energy G = H − TS, not energy. Splitting raises entropy (1 particle → 2); the free-energy minimum sits at a MIXTURE. At T>0 the uphill state is always populated at a nonzero fraction.

---

Q: State the Boltzmann intuition for equilibrium.
A: At temperature T, lower-energy states are favoured but not exclusive; higher states are populated ∝ e^(−ΔE/kT) (like a ball in a valley or the atmosphere). So some of every species always exists.

---

Q: Relate the equilibrium constant to free energy. What does its sign of ΔG° tell you?
A: K = e^(−ΔG°/RT). ΔG° < 0 → K > 1 (products favoured); ΔG° > 0 → K < 1 (reactants favoured); never exactly 0 or ∞ at finite T. A weak acid has ΔG° > 0 for dissociation.

---

Q: What does "equilibrium is dynamic" mean?
A: The forward and reverse reactions both keep happening at equal rates; concentrations sit still but nothing has stopped or been used up.

---

Q: How do cells make a thermodynamically unfavourable reaction happen?
A: Reaction coupling — link it to a very favourable reaction (e.g. ATP hydrolysis, large negative ΔG) so the summed ΔG is negative. Central trick of bioenergetics (M10).
